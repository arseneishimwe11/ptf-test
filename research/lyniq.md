# Lyniq ⭐ — lyniq.framer.website (favorite — studied closest)

**Positioning:** premium ($129) design-agency template by Anatolii; 11 prebuilt
pages on Framer CMS. Marketed on its *motion system*: splash screen, page
transitions, tickers, parallax, sticky scroll. The strongest overall reference
for "cinematic but disciplined."

## Palette & type
- Dark studio aesthetic: deep near-black ground, warm light text, restrained
  accent used for links/highlights *(reported "sleek", consistent with the
  agency-dark genre)*.
- Editorial pairing: display face for oversized statements + neutral grotesk for
  UI/body *(typical of the tier)*.

## Layout & rhythm
- Full-bleed hero statement → services → case studies → testimonials → stats →
  contact; sections separated by large type breaks rather than boxes *(reported
  sections list)*.
- Tickers/marquees used as section dividers and skill strips *(reported)*.

## Motion & interaction — the signature set *(all reported in listing/reviews)*
1. **Splash screen / preloader** with animation "optimized to work seamlessly
   across all browsers, even Safari," which "guides visitors smoothly into the
   Hero" — i.e. the loader exit and hero entrance are one choreographed sequence.
2. **Fluid page transitions** between CMS pages.
3. **Tickers** (infinite marquees), **parallax sections**, and **sticky scroll
   elements** — "motion without feeling decorative."
4. Case-study pages integrate a **video player** for motion work.

## What we take
- The loader→hero handoff as a single choreographed sequence (our loader counts
  up, wipes out, and hands its timing to the hero's staggered entrance).
- Sticky-scroll case-study presentation → our Work section's pinned/stacking
  cards.
- Marquee ticker as a section divider (skills strip).
- The discipline: every effect listed has a job (orientation, continuity,
  density-relief).

## What we skip
- Multi-page transitions (single-page site) — replaced by in-page section
  choreography.
- CMS/blog machinery.
